A classical bit is either 0 or 1. A qubit can be in a combination of both until we measure it. Dirac notation gives these states names, |0⟩ and |1⟩. Measurement forces the qubit to choose one result.Open full lesson →
Quantum gates change a qubit’s state. The Hadamard gate creates an even blend of 0 and 1, while the Pauli gates flip or rotate the state. The Bloch Sphere is a picture that helps us see these changes.Open full lesson →
When qubits work together, their states combine using a tensor product. Entangled qubits behave like one connected system, even when separated. A CNOT gate is a common way to create this connection.Open full lesson →
A quantum circuit is a recipe: wires represent qubits and symbols represent gates. Quantum operations must be reversible, so the information in a state cannot simply be erased before measurement.Open full lesson →
Phase is like the timing of a wave. Waves that line up become stronger, while waves that oppose each other cancel out. Quantum algorithms use this interference to make useful answers more likely.Open full lesson →
